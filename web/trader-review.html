What the evidence says

  • Exit timing is the biggest measurement problem. 21 of 21 exits had an earlier daily candle reaching the modeled stop or target. 3 first-crossing bars touched both. Daily opens miss intraday events and cannot establish execution order or achievable fills. Winners can also become losers under different intraday paths.
  • The current entries can chase jumps. 8 losing exits followed entries over 3% above the previous close, but so did 3 of the 4 winners. A cap may remove winners too. The current rule permits up to 15% and ranks candidates by largest extension above the 20-session high. Historical volume confirmation is a 10/30-session ratio, not that day's buying volume.
  • The fixed 3% stop is small relative to selected stocks' movement. 16 of 17 losers had a prior 14-session average true range above 3% of price. This suggests testing volatility-aware sizing/stops; it does not prove wider stops improve returns.
  • Costs add to losses but do not explain them. Removing estimated fees alone still leaves −₹28,387.23. The calculation still includes assumed slippage.

Fix order

  1. Validate execution first: collect/replay timestamped intraday candles or ticks and depth evidence, including gap handling, stale quotes and partial fills. Do not replace missing paths with guaranteed 3% exits.
  2. Keep the capped-entry candidate in research: require evidence from independent periods and forward paper runs before promoting it. Do not tune to erase this month's losses.
  3. Then test risk per trade: volatility-based sizing, stop distance and a portfolio risk cap together. Increasing stop distance alone increases the loss budget.
  4. Test confirmation and re-entry separately: current-session breakout confirmation and a cooldown after a stop. Historical volume and next-day re-entry currently permit repeated failed breakouts.
